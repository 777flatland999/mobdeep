My Cameras

Nikon CoolPix P1000

Nikon 1979 35mm FE

The Nikon COOLPIX P1000 is best understood as an exercise in extreme zoom-ratio optimization on a small sensor, where nearly every other optical parameter is traded away to achieve the range. The fixed NIKKOR lens runs from 4.3 to 539 mm physical focal length, which Nikon states as a 24–3000 mm field-of-view equivalent in 35 mm format; that is a true 125× optical zoom ratio. Maximum aperture scales from f/2.8 at wide to f/8 at full telephoto, controlled by a seven-blade electronic iris. In practice it functions as a specialized instrument for lunar, avian, and long-distance observation where reach outweighs resolving power.

The Nikon 35mm FE is best described as a precision electromechanical image-plane platform for Nikon F-mount optics. It was introduced in 1978, mine is from 1979. Its engineering logic is graceful degradation: if the battery fails, the camera still operates at the mechanical 1/90 s speed. But as a 1979 SLR, it is a compact, well-damped F-mount film plane with reliable TTL metering and a fail-safe mechanical shutter path.
